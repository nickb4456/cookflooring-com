Bathroom planning · Rhode Island homeowners

Plan the shower behind the tile, then choose the tile.

By Cook Flooring & Tile · Published October 2, 2026

Tile color is the visible decision. The preparation, water management, layout, and division of work deserve just as much attention. Use these questions to turn a bathroom idea into a clear installation scope.

In this guide
  1. Start with the boundaries of the project
  2. Ask how water is managed beneath the tile
  3. Review the layout before installation begins
  4. Plan for everyday use and maintenance
  5. Put the full scope in writing
  6. Agree on access and return to use

Start with the boundaries of the project

Write down whether you are changing the bathroom floor, a tub surround, an entire shower, or several of those areas. Mark what stays: the vanity, toilet, tub, doors, and existing fixtures. This gives everyone the same starting point before discussing tile quantities or labor.

Separate tile work from plumbing, electrical work, glass, painting, and ventilation. Ask who arranges each trade and what must be complete before the next stage can begin. An estimate for tile installation should not leave you guessing whether moving a valve or removing a vanity is included.

Ask how water is managed beneath the tile

Tile and grout alone do not form a waterproof shower. Schluter's shower guidance explains why moisture management belongs behind the tile surface. Ask the installer to identify the proposed system and how its parts work together.

  • Which shower walls, base, curb, and niches are included in the waterproofing scope?
  • How are seams, corners, pipe openings, and the connection to the drain addressed?
  • What inspection or water-testing steps are planned before the assembly is covered?
  • What records, product information, and progress photos will you receive?

The answer should describe your shower and its selected system. A grout sealer is not a substitute for a properly planned waterproofing assembly.

Review the layout before installation begins

Bring the actual tile size, edge trim, grout color, and any accent material to the planning conversation. Ask where the layout starts, where cuts will fall, and how the floor meets the wall. A sample board helps compare choices that are difficult to picture separately.

Discuss the niche position and shelf height in relation to the people using the shower. Ask how the niche lines up with tile joints and what happens if the wall framing limits the proposed location. Agree on changes before treating a sketch as a final layout.

For the shower floor, ask whether the chosen tile suits the base and drain layout. The drain and slope arrangement can affect tile size. Choose the tile and shower assembly together, with the product's intended use in mind.

Plan for everyday use and maintenance

Think through where towels, toiletries, and cleaning supplies will go. If you are considering a bench, grab bars, or a more accessible entrance, raise it before the walls are finished so support and layout can be planned. Discuss the needs of the people using the room instead of choosing features from a photo alone.

Ask for care instructions for the tile, grout, sealant, and any natural stone you choose. Confirm which products need ongoing maintenance and where movement joints or flexible sealant are part of the design. Keep those instructions with your project records.

Put the full scope in writing

  • Removal: identify surfaces to be removed, debris handling, and protection for the route through the house.
  • Preparation: state the included substrate work and how hidden damage will be assessed and priced.
  • Materials: name who supplies tile, waterproofing components, setting materials, trim, grout, and sealant.
  • Details: include niches, shelves, thresholds, edge treatment, and the limits of tiled areas.
  • Handoffs: assign plumbing, glass, fixtures, and other work that sits outside the tile scope.

Agree on access and return to use

If this is the home's only bathroom, discuss access before work starts. Ask when fixtures will be unavailable, what depends on other trades, and when the selected materials allow the shower to return to service. Use the actual product requirements and work sequence for that plan.
