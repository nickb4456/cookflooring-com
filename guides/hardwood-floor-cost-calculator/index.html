    How much does it cost to refinish hardwood floors?

    In Rhode Island, sanding and refinishing a hardwood floor typically runs about $4 to $6 per square foot with a clear finish. A 300 square foot living and dining room comes to about $1,200 to $1,800.

    A stain color adds about $0.75 to $1.25 per square foot, because it adds a step. Stairs are priced by the step, about $50 to $80 each, because every step is sanded by hand. Sanding and refinishing usually costs less than replacing the floor.

    What moves a refinishing price

    • Board repairs. Damaged boards are repaired or replaced before sanding.
    • Deep wear and pet stains. Sanding does not remove every deep stain, so some boards may need replacing.
    • Carpet or adhesive. Anything covering the wood has to come up first.
    • Color. A stain adds a step and makes color the main decision.
    • Stairs. Each step is sanded by hand, so stairs are priced by the step.

    How much is hardwood flooring installed, per square foot?

    Most hardwood installs run about $6 to $12 per square foot installed, depending on the wood, your subfloor, and the layout. For a 300 square foot room that is about $1,800 to $3,600.

    That range is wide because the wood itself varies so much, and because two rooms of the same size can need very different preparation. The calculator above shows the full range for your square footage. The walkthrough is where it narrows to one number.

    What decides where you land in that range

    • The wood. Species, solid or engineered, plank width, and prefinished or finished on site.
    • The subfloor. A flat, sound subfloor is quick. Repairs and leveling add work.
    • The layout. Hallways, closets, angles, and doorways mean more cuts than one open room.
    • Removal. Taking up and hauling away the old floor is its own line.
    • Transitions and trim. Where the new floor meets tile, stairs, and baseboards.

    Flooring is ordered with extra for cuts and waste, commonly 5 to 10 percent, and more for diagonal or patterned layouts. Your written estimate states the quantity. See our hardwood installation service for how we plan layout and transitions.

    Glue-down wood flooring installation

    Glue-down is how hardwood is installed when there is no wood subfloor to nail into, most often over a concrete slab. It adds about $1 to $2 per square foot to an install, so glue-down hardwood typically runs about $7 to $14 per square foot installed.

    The installer trowels flooring adhesive onto the slab and sets each board into it. It is the usual way to put engineered hardwood over concrete, including basements and additions built on a slab. Solid three-quarter inch hardwood is normally nailed to a wood subfloor and is not recommended below ground level. Cook Flooring & Tile installs hardwood both ways: nailed to a wood subfloor, or glued down over concrete.

    What a glue-down job involves

    1. Test the concrete for moisture before any wood is ordered.
    2. Flatten the slab. High spots are ground down and low spots are filled.
    3. Apply a moisture barrier where the adhesive system calls for one.
    4. Trowel the adhesive in sections and set the boards into it.
    5. Keep off the floor for the cure time the adhesive maker gives.

    The adhesive and the slab work are what add to the price. A slab that needs a lot of flattening or moisture control costs more. Planning a basement? Read the best basement floor starts with the concrete under it.

    How to measure your floor

    1. Measure the length and the width of each room in feet. Round up to the next half foot.
    2. Multiply length by width. A 12 by 15 foot room is 180 square feet.
    3. For an L-shaped room, split it into two rectangles and add them.
    4. Add closets and hallways as their own rectangles.
    5. Leave furniture in the count. Take out areas that will not get the floor, such as a hearth or a kitchen island base.

    A tape measure number is enough for a ballpark. We take exact measurements at the walkthrough.

    What a calculator cannot see

    Two floors of the same size can be very different jobs. A calculator works from square feet. It cannot see the condition of the subfloor, moisture in a slab, damage hidden under carpet, or how many times an old floor has already been sanded.

    That is what the walkthrough is for. We look at the floor, talk through the options, and put the scope in a written, itemized estimate. Our flooring estimate checklist shows what a complete estimate should cover, so you can compare more than one.

    Hardwood floor cost questions

    How much does it cost to refinish hardwood floors?

    In Rhode Island, sanding and refinishing typically runs about $4 to $6 per square foot with a clear finish. A stain color adds about $0.75 to $1.25 per square foot, and stairs run about $50 to $80 per step. A 300 square foot room comes to about $1,200 to $1,800.

    How much does it cost to refinish 1,000 square feet of hardwood?

    About $4,000 to $6,000 with a clear finish, or about $4,750 to $7,250 with a stain color. Stairs, board repairs, and carpet removal are added to that.

    About $6,000 to $12,000, depending on the wood, your subfloor, and the layout. Removing an old floor is priced separately.

    Is it cheaper to refinish or replace hardwood floors?

    Sanding and refinishing usually costs less than replacing the floor. Refinishing typically runs about $4 to $6 per square foot. New hardwood typically runs about $6 to $12 per square foot installed. Refinishing is only an option when the existing wood is sound and has enough material left to sand.

    Glue-down adds about $1 to $2 per square foot to an install for the adhesive and slab preparation, so it typically runs about $7 to $14 per square foot installed. A slab that needs a lot of flattening or moisture control costs more.
