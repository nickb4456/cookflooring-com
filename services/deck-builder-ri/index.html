Repair or rebuild?

Start with the part of the deck you cannot see from across the yard.

The useful decision is based on framing condition, connections, stairs, railings, and surface wear—not appearance alone.

Surface wear

Boards need replacement

If the framing remains sound, replacing worn surface boards can renew the deck without tearing out usable structure.

Localized damage

Rot or framing repairs

Joists, rim areas, stairs, or other damaged sections can be evaluated individually before deciding how much of the deck needs replacement.

Broader failure

Rebuild what is not sound

When the underlying structure no longer makes sense to preserve, the estimate can shift toward a safer rebuild rather than cosmetic repairs.

Rhode Island deck repair questions

A useful estimate begins with the framing, not an assumption that every old deck needs a full replacement.

Can an existing deck be repaired instead of rebuilt?

Often, yes. We inspect the framing, posts, stairs, railings, and surface boards first, then explain which parts are sound and which parts need repair or replacement.

Do you work with composite and pressure-treated decking?

Yes. We work with pressure-treated lumber, cedar, and composite deck boards, including framing repairs, surface replacement, stairs, and railings.

Where do you work on decks in Rhode Island?

We serve communities including Cranston, Providence, Warwick, Coventry, East Greenwich, North Kingstown, Johnston, and surrounding towns.

Do you inspect framing before quoting new boards?

Yes. We look at the structural condition first so a surface replacement is not used to hide framing that should be repaired.
