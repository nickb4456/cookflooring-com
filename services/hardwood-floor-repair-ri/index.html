Matching the old floor

We match the species, the board width, and the thickness. Many older floors are 2¼ inch oak strips, which are still easy to find. Unusual widths or old reclaimed wood can take longer to source, and we tell you before work starts.

Why the finish matters

New boards look different from boards that have aged for years until they are sanded and finished together. For the most even look, refinish the whole room or stop the repair at a natural break like a doorway.

Fix the cause first

Water stains and cupping come from somewhere: a leak, a damp basement, or a worn door seal. That source needs to be fixed and the floor dry before new wood goes in, or the damage comes back.

Engineered floors

Engineered hardwood can often be repaired the same way. Keep the product name or a spare box if you have one. It helps match the color and the thickness of the top layer.

Hardwood floor repair questions

The first question is whether the rest of the floor is worth keeping. Usually it is.

How much does hardwood floor repair cost in Rhode Island?

A small repair of a few boards usually runs about $400 to $750. A larger section runs about $800 to $1,800, and a water-damaged area about $1,500 to $4,000. Refinishing the whole room so the repair blends in adds about $4 to $6 per square foot. Your written quote after the walkthrough sets the price.

Can you replace just a few boards?

Yes. We cut out the damaged boards and weave new ones in so the joints stagger like the rest of the floor. A small repair can be finished on its own, or together with the whole room for the closest match.

Will the repair match my floor?

We match the species, the board width, and the thickness. New wood and old wood take finish differently, so the closest match comes from sanding and finishing the room together. At the walkthrough we tell you how close a spot repair alone will look.

Can water-damaged hardwood be saved?

Sometimes. Boards that dry back flat may only need sanding. Boards that stay cupped, buckled, or black need to come out. The leak has to be fixed and the subfloor dry before new wood goes in.

Should I repair my hardwood floor or replace it?

If most of the floor is sound, repairing and refinishing usually costs far less than a new floor. If the damage runs through several rooms, compare both. Our guide on refinishing or replacing hardwood floors walks through the choice.
