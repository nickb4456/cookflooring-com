Choosing the wood

Red oak or white oak for a Rhode Island home?

Oak runs through a large share of older New England houses, and it remains the most widely used hardwood flooring in the country. The two oaks look and behave differently, so settle on the species before you pick a stain.

Adding on to an existing floor

New boards read as one floor with the old ones when the species, width, and thickness are the same. Identify the existing wood first, because red and white oak take the same stain differently. A new floor may not exactly match an older floor's color or grain. Where an exact match is unlikely, a planned transition at a doorway often looks better than a near miss.

Solid or engineered hardwood

Solid hardwood is one piece of wood from top to bottom. It suits rooms on or above ground level and can be sanded and refinished many times. Engineered hardwood has a real wood surface over a layered core, moves less with humidity, and can go on any level of the house, including a basement. How often it can be refinished depends on the thickness of the top layer.

Strip or wide plank

Narrow strip flooring, about 2¼ inches wide, is common in older Rhode Island homes. Wider planks show more of the grain. They also move more with the seasons, so the installation method and steady indoor humidity matter more as boards get wider.

Wood and New England seasons

Wood shrinks during the heating season and swells in humid summers. Small gaps that open in winter and close again in summer are normal for a solid wood floor. The National Wood Flooring Association suggests keeping a home between 60 and 80 degrees and 30 to 50 percent humidity year round to limit that movement.

Hardwood installation questions

The condition of the room and the transitions between spaces matter as much as the flooring itself.

What happens before hardwood installation begins?

We check the subfloor, plan board direction and transitions, and rack the boards so color and grain are distributed before installation.

Do you install solid and engineered hardwood?

Yes. We install solid and engineered hardwood, including red oak, white oak, maple, and prefinished planks.

Is red oak or white oak better for hardwood floors?

Both are durable choices for a home. White oak is slightly harder, resists water a little better, and suits natural, light, and gray finishes. Red oak has a warmer cast and bolder grain, takes medium and dark stains well, and is often the right match for an existing red oak floor.

Can new hardwood match the floors already in my house?

It can come close when the species, board width, and thickness are the same. We review the existing boards, the floor height, and the finish at the walkthrough. A new floor may not exactly match an older floor's color or grain, so a planned transition is sometimes the better choice.
